## New Anchor Profiles (v2.0 Additions)

### ⭐ Edward Tufte — ET Notebooks (DS2) — Score: 9.0

**Scores: PV=10 / FD=10 / CP=7 / CC=10 / CU=8**

**PV=10:** Tufte's notebooks are his own voice, in his own words, freely published. This is the highest-integrity anchor in the entire set for primary voice — it is not a summary of his books but the man directly. His framing of the rage-to-conclude bias, the data-ink ratio, chartjunk taxonomy, sparklines, and XY path charts are articulated with precision that no secondary source matches.

**FD=10:** Tufte contributes at least six distinct, retrievable mental models: chartjunk (3-way taxonomy), data-ink ratio (operationalized as erase/erase-redundant passes), rage-to-conclude bias (Interocular Trauma Test), sparklines (concept + design spec), XY path charts (explicit non-time-series alternative), and the data density principle. No other anchor in the set contributes this many named, citable frameworks.

**CP=7:** The ET Notebooks contain internal tension — Tufte has been criticized by the empirical community (Franconeri, Bateman et al.) for over-generalizing from aesthetic preference to universal law. The notebooks don't surface this self-critique. However, his *inference bias* notebook explicitly argues against the common pattern-seeking failure mode, which is itself a form of pushback against naive visualization practice. Partial credit.

**CC=10:** All ET Notebook content is freely published at edwardtufte.com. Day 2 explicitly notes this is read-and-retrieve, using principles summarized and key phrases quoted with attribution. Zero book-reproduction risk. Full compliance.

**CU=8:** The core principles (data-ink ratio, chartjunk, sparklines) are durable and still-cited. Some of Tufte's specific prescriptions (e.g., extreme minimalism, anti-grid stance) have been partially challenged by empirical work showing that grid lines aid quantitative reading in some contexts. Currency is high for the principles; slightly lower for specific prescriptions that have been refined.

**What Tufte adds that no other anchor covers:** The only anchor in the set that explicitly names and operationalizes *graphical integrity as a moral/epistemic obligation* — not just a design preference. His inference-bias framework (rage-to-conclude, Interocular Trauma Test) is unique and directly useful for any chart that will be used to support a decision.

---

### ⭐ The Pudding — Production Pipeline (DS1) — Score: 8.6

**Scores: PV=8 / FD=9 / CP=6 / CC=10 / CU=10**

**PV=8:** The Pudding's documented process articles (written by Amber Thomas and Matt Daniels) have a clear, distinctive voice — experimental, honest about failure, craft-first. The Continue/Pivot/Put It Down framework, the question-uniqueness test, and the documentation of killed stories are genuinely first-person. The only reason this doesn't reach 9–10 is that the process documentation is written for practitioners explaining their own workflow, not for a broader audience; it reads as insider documentation, which slightly limits the primary-voice depth.

**FD=9:** The Pudding fills a genuine gap: no other anchor in the set documents the *pre-production* workflow — from question ideation through data feasibility, ethical review, storyboarding, and the kill decision. The Continue/Pivot/Put It Down decision framework is a distinct mental model that is retrievable and actionable. Their "audience, scope, complexity, structure" anatomy for a data story is a four-part framework not duplicated elsewhere in the set.

**CP=6:** The process articles are largely self-affirming — they describe how The Pudding works, with real examples of killed stories, but they don't include tension about whether their own approach is optimal or limitations of scrollytelling as a communication form. The absence of self-critique is expected (these are internal process documents, not academic papers) but it limits the pushback score.

**CC=10:** All content is freely published at pudding.cool. Fully agent-readable. Zero compliance risk.

**CU=10:** The Pudding is actively maintained and producing new work. Their process is current and relevant to data storytelling in 2026. Unlike some foundational books that describe tools no longer in common use, The Pudding's workflow is tool-agnostic and applicable regardless of whether the output is D3, Svelte, Datawrapper, or Flourish.

**What The Pudding adds that no other anchor covers:** The only anchor that treats story-killing as a core design skill. The pre-production decision framework (uniqueness test, data feasibility test, ethics test) fills a gap that every other resource in the set implicitly skips — they all assume you already have a valid story and are choosing how to visualize it. The Pudding starts one step earlier.

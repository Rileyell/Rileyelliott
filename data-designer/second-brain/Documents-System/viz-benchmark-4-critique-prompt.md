# Data Designer Benchmark 4 — Chart Critique Test

You are Riley Voss, Data Designer.

A client has forwarded you the chart below — built by their internal analyst — and asked: *"We're trying to decide whether to expand into APAC or LATAM next year. This is the chart our analyst made. Does it help us answer that question?"*

---

## The Chart (Described in Full)

**Title:** 2024 Revenue by Segment  
**Type:** 3D pie chart with exaggerated tilt perspective  
**Number of slices:** 7  
**Legend:** External, positioned to the right, using color matching to identify each slice  
**Labels:** Percentages printed directly on each slice in 6pt font  

**Slices (clockwise from top):**

| Segment | % of Revenue | Category |
|---|---|---|
| North America | 38% | Geographic Region |
| Europe | 22% | Geographic Region |
| APAC | 18% | Geographic Region |
| Direct Sales | 10% | Sales Channel |
| LATAM | 7% | Geographic Region |
| Channel Partners | 4% | Sales Channel |
| MEA | 1% | Geographic Region |

The 3D perspective places North America and Europe at the front of the tilt (making them appear largest) and APAC, LATAM, and MEA at the back/right side (making them appear compressed).

---

## What You Must Deliver

**1. Name two distinct problems with this chart that specifically compromise the APAC vs. LATAM expansion decision.** For each problem: name it precisely, explain the specific wrong conclusion the client might reach because of it, and tie it to the APAC/LATAM comparison — not just "this is a general best practice violation."

**2. Identify the single most important fix.** One change that, if made first, would most dramatically improve this chart's usefulness for the client's decision. Explain why this fix matters more than the others.

**3. Describe the replacement chart.** Provide a full encoding spec: chart type, x-axis, y-axis, color handling, and a title that is a finding — not a label.

**4. Name one thing the analyst got right.** Find something defensible in their design decisions, even if the execution failed.

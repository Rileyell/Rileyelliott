## Top 3 Strongest Anchors

**1. FT Visual Vocabulary (9.2)**
The clearest thinking tool in the set. Starts from data relationship, not chart type — exactly the right direction of reasoning. Fully free, GitHub-hosted, constantly referenced by practitioners. Maximum currency, maximum distinctiveness, and it has an embedded critique (forces you to name the relationship before picking a form, which rejects default chart selection).

**2. Cleveland & McGill 1984 (9.0)**
The empirical foundation under every encoding decision. The perceptual accuracy ranking is one of the few genuinely falsifiable claims in visualization — you can cite it, it holds up, it has been replicated and extended. Zero compliance risk (pre-1984 JASA paper; accessible as a free PDF with verified link). Critical intellectual backbone.

**3. Franconeri et al. (2021) / Nightingale — Data Viz Society (8.8, tie)**
Franconeri: the best modern synthesis of empirical viz research in a practitioner-readable format. Covers uncertainty communication and audience diversity that the foundational books underweight. Nightingale: the only living, community-maintained journal in the set — covers ethics, accessibility, and practitioner discourse that static books can't. Both are future-proof and high in pushback/critique.

## Specific Fixes for Anchors Scoring Below 8.0 Average

### Visual Cinnamon — Bremer (7.8) — Fix: Add critique layer
**Problem:** The anchor celebrates Bremer's work as a ceiling/north star but includes no tension or limitation note. Her work skews heavily toward bespoke, high-effort D3 visualizations — not a realistic model for most production work.
**Fix:** Add an explicit note: "Bremer's work is aspirational — budget 80% more time than a standard chart. Her pattern value is the *iteration discipline*, not the visual complexity." This makes the anchor instructive rather than purely inspirational.

### PolicyViz — Schwabish (7.8) — Fix: Sharpen the distinctive framework
**Problem:** Schwabish is positioned as "the Tufte heir" but the anchor doesn't articulate a specific mental model or heuristic that is uniquely his. It reads as "good practitioner blog" without a concrete lens.
**Fix:** Anchor to his specific typology from *Better Data Visualizations* — particularly his chart taxonomy by relationship type (which predates and rivals the FT Vocabulary). Cite one concrete technique (e.g., the SIFT method for redesigns) as the retrievable handle.

### Visualising Data — Andy Kirk (7.6) — Fix: Operationalize the Explore/Explain distinction
**Problem:** The anchor names the Explore/Explain decision as foundational but doesn't give a decision rule — it's referenced as a concept without a retrievable rubric.
**Fix:** Add the explicit trigger: "Ask at the start of every project: *Is this chart for the analyst or for the decision-maker?* Exploratory = density, multiple views, filtering. Explanatory = single claim, cleared axis, headline caption." Then the anchor is actionable, not just conceptual.

### Munzner — Visualization Analysis & Design (7.6) — Fix: Expand the access note + critique layer
**Problem:** The anchor correctly flags that the full book isn't free, but the embedded mental models (marks+channels, nested model) are among the most rigorous in the set and the anchor doesn't give enough voice to Munzner's distinctive framework. Also, no critique: her model is highly abstract and rarely applicable to fast production decisions.
**Fix:** (a) Surface the marks+channels framework explicitly as a retrievable rubric. (b) Add: "Munzner is the right reference when justifying *why* a fundamentally different visualization idiom was chosen — not for routine chart-type selection, where the FT Vocabulary is faster."

### FlowingData — Yau (7.2) — Fix: Tighten scope + critique paywall limitation
**Problem:** The paywall caveat is noted but the anchor doesn't compensate by naming a specific Yau heuristic or distinctive technique. "Longest-running high-quality blog" is a reputation claim, not a reasoning tool.
**Fix:** (a) Name Yau's specific contribution: his statistical chart vocabulary — uncertainty bands, violin plots, survival curves — that most design-focused blogs ignore. (b) Add: "Access limited to annual best-of and blog; tutorials are paywalled. Treat as a signals/inspiration layer, not a production reference."

### Ware — Perception for Design (7.2) — Fix: Free access problem + voice depth
**Problem:** The book is not freely accessible (only Chapter 4 is), and the anchor's primary voice is thin — it's described correctly but without a retrievable heuristic that Ware specifically contributes beyond what Cleveland & McGill already covers.
**Fix:** Restrict the anchor's stated use case to: "Color and preattentive processing decisions only." Add: "For quantitative encoding decisions, Cleveland & McGill is faster and more directly applicable." Acknowledge that Ware's full framework is inaccessible and consider substituting with a freely accessible perception paper (e.g., Franconeri already covers much of the same ground).

### Information Is Beautiful — McCandless (6.8) — Fix: Critique the style + sharpen the four-factor lens
**Problem:** McCandless is cited approvingly but the anchor doesn't acknowledge the frequent criticism that his work prioritizes visual spectacle over perceptual accuracy — a real tension for a persona committed to accuracy-first design.
**Fix:** Add explicit tension: "McCandless's work is frequently criticized for sacrificing accuracy for aesthetic effect (see B3 Franconeri). Use his four-factor lens and scale for aspirational scope only; don't use his outputs as templates for production charts. The value is the question he asks, not the answer he gives."

### Harvard / Duke / MIT Syllabi (6.6) — Fix: Convert to meta-index role or drop
**Problem:** Three academic syllabus links add redundancy without depth. They point to Wilke (already A3), Munzner (already A1), and D3/Observable (not in the persona's primary stack). No distinctive voice, no critique, no retrievable heuristic. They exist as "this is what academia assigns" signals — useful once, not in daily use.
**Fix (Option A — Compress):** Collapse all three into a single "Academic Curriculum" note, keeping only the single best reading-list pointer (MIT OCW 6.C35 has the best free material coverage including D'Ignazio's data ethics lens). Gains: removes 2 low-value anchors, keeps the meta-reference function.
**Fix (Option B — Replace):** Swap one syllabus slot for a missing practical anchor — e.g., **Observable Plot documentation** (the emerging standard for exploratory viz with a free, interactive notebook environment) or **The Grammar of Graphics — Wilkinson** (the conceptual framework under ggplot2 and Vega-Lite, retrievable as a specific mental model).
